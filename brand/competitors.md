# Red White and Clix — Competitor Brand Analysis

*Reviewed September 21, 2026 from current official websites. Per client direction, the competitor set includes only organizations serving veterans.*

## Primary aspirational benchmark

Wesley Robertson identified **Stack Up** as the organization he initially wanted Red White and Clix to emulate. Treat Stack Up as the main aspirational benchmark, not merely another competitor. The relevant lessons are its clear program architecture, gaming-native veteran mission, multiple participation routes and strong transparency practice. RWC should translate those strengths into a smaller, tabletop-specific Midwest model rather than copy Stack Up's national video-gaming identity.

## 01 — Landscape

The category splits between veteran-service institutions, participatory gratitude programs and gaming-for-veterans communities. Veteran brands often use patriotic palettes, heroic language, portraits and donation-first calls to action. RWC’s open territory is a produced, in-person tabletop experience with local human scale and veteran-founded purpose.

## 02 — Profiles

### Stack Up — https://www.stackup.org/

**Does:** Supports veterans through gaming and geek culture programs.  
**Positioning:** Gaming as positive mental-health and suicide-prevention infrastructure.  
**Audience:** Veterans, gamers, streamers, volunteers, sponsors.  
**Core message:** “Combating veteran suicide through gaming.”  
**Personality:** Tactical, passionate, community-driven.  
**Identity:** Red/black military-gaming aesthetic, strong program names, gamer imagery.  
**Strengths:** Tight mission/category fit, multiple programs, clear ways to help.  
**Weaknesses:** Broad program system can feel complex; militarized naming may not welcome everyone.  
**Owned territory:** National gaming support for veterans.  
**RWC difference:** Live tabletop competition, Midwest event community, smaller and more personal.

### Operation Gratitude — https://www.operationgratitude.com/

**Does:** Mobilizes volunteers to create and deliver care packages and acts of gratitude.  
**Positioning:** A tangible way for every American to thank those who serve.  
**Core line:** “Saying Thank You to All Who Serve.”  
**Personality:** Warm, grateful, inclusive.  
**Identity:** Polished national nonprofit; human photography and impact numbers.  
**Strengths:** Clear participation mechanism, impressive proof, broad volunteer routes.  
**Weaknesses:** Gratitude is broad rather than gaming-specific; less focused on peer community among participants.  
**Owned territory:** Gratitude made tangible.  
**RWC difference:** Camaraderie and competition made purposeful.

### Wounded Warrior Project — https://www.woundedwarriorproject.org/

**Does:** Provides broad programs, services, advocacy, and community for post-9/11 wounded veterans and families.  
**Positioning:** Comprehensive lifetime empowerment.  
**Personality:** Authoritative, supportive, institutional.  
**Identity:** Black/orange, polished portraits, program depth, strong calls to action.  
**Strengths:** Scale, recognition, service breadth, proof infrastructure.  
**Weaknesses:** Institutional distance and complexity; not built around gamer identity.  
**Owned territory:** Comprehensive post-9/11 warrior services.  
**RWC difference:** RWC is an event/community fundraiser and connector, not a direct-service substitute.

## 03 — Brand map

**Axis 1:** Donation/service-led ←→ participation/play-led  
**Axis 2:** national/institutional ←→ local/personal

WWP sits service-led and national. Operation Gratitude is participatory but national and volunteer-led. Stack Up is participation-led, national and gamer-native. RWC’s strongest territory is **participation-led + local/personal + veteran-founded**.

## 04 — Messaging patterns

**Table stakes:** “support our heroes,” “make an impact,” “community,” “give back.” None differentiate alone.  
**Avoid:** unsupported suicide-prevention outcomes; generic “heroes” language; flag-heavy appeals without program detail; “every dollar” claims without accounting.  
**Underused space:** the quality of the in-room experience; belonging without pity; clear event-to-beneficiary reporting.

## 05 — Visual patterns

Patriotic red/white/blue is crowded. Veteran institutions often use solemn portraits; gaming brands use dark tactical treatments or neon digital motifs. RWC can retain its recognizable colors while differentiating through bright documentary tabletop photography, visible hands/boards/tables, readable logistics, and a less militarized interface. Avoid AI military art as primary proof.

## 06 — Opportunities

1. **Own “the purposeful table.”** Make every table the visual and verbal unit of community.
2. **Prove event quality.** Schedules, formats, beginner paths, venue details, and organizer credibility.
3. **Report the handoff.** Show what was raised, who benefited, and what happened after the event.
4. **Welcome without flattening.** Speak to competitive players, veterans, newcomers, and sponsors through separate pathways.
5. **Build regional equity.** Become the Midwest annual event people put on their calendar, then extend carefully.

## 07 — Implications

Position RWC as a veteran-founded tabletop event community, not a small imitation of a national veteran charity. Lead with participation and belonging; substantiate the veteran impact. Use the existing palette as recognition, but let authentic event photography and precise information carry the brand.
