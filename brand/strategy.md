# Red White and Clix — Brand Strategy

*Working strategy built from Wesley Robertson’s survey, the live site, public Facebook content, current event listings, and verified IRS records. Strategic recommendations require client approval before public launch.*

## Primary aspirational benchmark

Stack Up is the founder's original model for what RWC could become. The strategic goal is to emulate its organizational clarity, gaming-based community model, participation options and transparent reporting while narrowing the expression to HeroClix, tabletop events and Midwest relationships. This is a benchmark for operating principles, not a direction to imitate its visual identity or claim its program breadth.

---

## 01 — WHAT IS RED WHITE AND CLIX?

> **Play gives people a reason to gather.**

Red White and Clix grew from Wesley Robertson’s experience of military service, loss, disability, and the practical difference that veteran support can make. Its conviction is simple: support does not have to begin with a formal appeal. It can begin around a table, through a shared hobby, where connection feels natural and participation has purpose.

The organization produces large tabletop gaming events, centered on HeroClix, that combine organized competition, community, merchandise, fundraising, and awareness for veteran-focused causes. It also connects visitors with veteran resources. The experience should feel credible enough for sponsors, welcoming enough for a first-time attendee, and meaningful without turning veterans’ lives into fundraising imagery.

---

## 02 — BRAND VISION

To become the Midwest’s most trusted veteran-founded tabletop event community — built on excellent play, real belonging, accountable support, and a model that can grow without losing its human scale.

**Belonging · Play · Service · Trust**

---

## 03 — BRAND MISSION

Red White and Clix brings gamers, veterans, families, sponsors, and supporters together through well-run tabletop events that create camaraderie and direct attention and resources toward veteran-focused causes.

**Gather · Compete · Connect · Support**

---

## 04 — BRAND VALUES

**Belonging by Design.** Events should make newcomers, regular players, veterans, and families feel that there is a place for them. Inclusion is an operating standard, not a slogan.

**Play With Purpose.** The game must be genuinely enjoyable and competently run. The cause adds meaning to the experience; it does not excuse a weak event.

**Service Without Spectacle.** Veterans are people with agency, humor, skill, and community. RWC avoids pity, partisan framing, and unverified claims about lives changed.

**Transparent Impact.** Fees, beneficiaries, donation allocation, and outcomes should be easy to understand and supportable with records.

**Camaraderie in Action.** Participation, volunteering, sponsoring, and returning each year turn a room of players into a durable community.

**Welcome · Purpose · Respect · Accountability**

---

## 05 — BRAND GOALS

**Fill the 2026 event:** Reach at least 80 clearly defined registrations for November 7–8 through one accurate registration path.

**Double qualified traffic:** Establish the baseline and grow visits that reach event, sponsor, donate, or resource actions—not traffic for its own sake.

**Make maintenance sustainable:** Build a site and content rhythm a small organization can update without specialist help for every change.

**Prove the impact:** Publish beneficiary, allocation, and event-outcome information that sponsors and donors can trust.

**Prepare the second event:** Define its purpose, economics, format, audience, and relationship to the annual flagship before naming or promoting it.

**Grow recognizable equity:** Use one name, one current domain, a consistent logo system, and a repeatable event architecture across touchpoints.

## 06 — TARGET AUDIENCE

RWC serves adults who want competition and camaraderie to mean something beyond the standings. The core is Midwest tabletop gamers; veterans and military families are a vital community segment, while sponsors and volunteers enable scale.

**Age:** primarily 18–55 · **Location:** Indiana/Midwest, with national reach potential · **Income:** broad; price sensitivity varies · **Intent:** event fee, travel, merchandise, donation, sponsorship · **City focus:** Lafayette/West Lafayette first, then Midwest drive markets

**Marcus, 34 — Competitive player, Indianapolis.** Marcus plans weekends around major HeroClix events. He wants clear formats, credible organizers, worthwhile competition, and a community he is proud to support. RWC gives him a serious tournament with a human reason to return.

**Dana, 43 — Veteran and casual gamer, Lafayette.** Dana wants connection without being treated as a symbol or a project. RWC offers a shared activity where veteran identity is respected but does not have to dominate every conversation.

**Priya, 39 — Community partnerships manager, regional employer.** Priya needs a local cause, a credible activation, clear sponsor benefits, and a defensible account of where support goes. RWC gives her an event people can attend, volunteer at, photograph, and understand.

---

## 07 — BRAND PERSONALITY

**Energetic · Human · Accessible · Purposeful · Bold**

RWC is the organized host who knows the rules, welcomes a new player, keeps the room moving, and remembers why everyone gathered. It can be playful about gaming and serious about safety, logistics, money, and veteran claims. Bold colors and competitive energy should never become aggression, militarized bravado, or partisan theater.

---

## 08 — BRAND VOICE

RWC speaks with warm conviction — welcoming, clear, energetic, grounded, and respectful.

**Inviting:** Opens the door without testing whether someone is a “real” gamer. **Specific:** Gives dates, formats, fees, venue, and next action. **Hopeful:** Shows what participation enables without promising outcomes it cannot prove. **Game-literate:** Uses HeroClix terms accurately and explains them when needed. **Veteran-respectful:** Centers dignity and agency. **Accountable:** States what is known and corrects outdated information.

---

## 09 — COMPETITIVE CONTEXT

**Stack Up** owns gaming as a national veteran mental-health and support platform, backed by multiple programs. RWC is narrower, live-event-led, Midwest-rooted, and centered on tabletop competition.

**Operation Gratitude** owns tangible volunteer gratitude at national scale. RWC’s participation mechanism is shared play and recurring event community.

**Wounded Warrior Project** owns comprehensive post-9/11 veteran services and advocacy. RWC should collaborate with or refer to qualified service organizations rather than imitate direct-service claims.

The competitive opening is not “another veteran charity.” It is **the veteran-founded tabletop event where serious play creates belonging and support.**

---

## 10 — POSITIONING

Most veteran nonprofits lead with need and donation; most gaming events lead with format and prizes. RWC can own the bridge: a well-run tabletop event where the experience is worth attending and the purpose gives attendance more meaning.

**Positioning statement:** For tabletop gamers, veterans, and community supporters who want their time together to matter, Red White and Clix is the veteran-founded event community that turns competitive play into camaraderie and support for veteran causes, because the organization combines lived experience, organized events, and an inclusive table.

On a map from **donation-first to participation-first** and **national/institutional to local/human**, RWC belongs in the participation-first, local/human corner. Stack Up is participation-led but national; WWP and Operation Gratitude are broader and more institutional.

---

## 11 — HOW RWC STANDS OUT

> **The event is not a wrapper for the mission; participation is the mission in motion.**

RWC does not ask people to choose between a credible gaming experience and a meaningful cause. It commits to both. That combination is difficult to copy because it depends on organizer trust, lived veteran context, game-community knowledge, and the operational ability to bring people back.

RWC should refuse inflated impact language, generic flag imagery, and a website that makes visitors reconcile conflicting logistics. Its distinction becomes credible when the experience is clear, welcoming, and transparently connected to beneficiary outcomes.

---

## 12 — TAGLINE DIRECTIONS

**Play Together. Stand With Veterans.** — Recommended; clear participation and mission without claiming direct clinical impact.

**One Community. One Mission.** — Existing line; emotionally strong and already in use, but less distinctive without supporting context.

**Where Play Builds Belonging.** — Leads with the community outcome and extends beyond fundraising.

**Game On. Give Back.** — Memorable and energetic, though similar in structure to category language used by other gaming fundraisers.

**Every Table Has a Purpose.** — Flexible for event, sponsor, and volunteer storytelling.
