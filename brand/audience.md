# Red White and Clix — Target Audience

## 01 — Audience overview

The primary audience is not “everyone who supports veterans.” It is Midwest tabletop gamers who will travel, pay, compete, and return for a well-run event—and who value a welcoming community and a credible veteran mission. Veterans and military families overlap this group but should never be treated as a single mindset. Sponsors, volunteers, donors, and resource seekers are separate decision audiences with different needs.

## 02 — Primary profile

**Demographics:** Adults 18–55; all genders; Indiana and Midwest drive markets first; mixed incomes and occupations; hobby spending is discretionary and price-sensitive when travel is required.

**Psychographics:** They value fair competition, belonging, collection/mastery, shared rituals, and organizers who understand the game. They distrust vague logistics, cliques, performative charity, and organizations that cannot explain where money goes.

**Behavior:** They discover events through Facebook, HCUnits, gaming stores, podcasts, Discords, friends, and tournament communities. They compare format, date, travel, entry fee, prize structure, organizer reputation, and who else is attending. Registration friction or conflicting prices creates immediate doubt.

## 03 — Personas

### Marcus, 34 — Competitive HeroClix player, Indianapolis

*“Give me the format, fee, schedule, prize structure, and a reason this event is worth the drive.”*

Marcus follows releases, tournament results, creators, and regional events. He may support the mission, but event credibility gets him through the door.

**Goals:** Strong competition; predictable logistics; community recognition.  
**Frustrations:** Conflicting prices; stale event pages; unclear registration counts.  
**Needs:** One authoritative event page, transparent rules, timely updates.  
**Triggers:** Trusted organizers, friends attending, useful prizes, a clear schedule.  
**Hesitations:** Long travel, ambiguous fees, amateur operations.  
**Channels:** HCUnits, Facebook groups/pages, stores, podcasts, Discord, YouTube.  
**RWC role:** A credible regional tournament with an additional reason to care.

### Dana, 43 — Veteran, parent, and casual tabletop player, Lafayette

*“I want to be around people who understand community without making my service the only thing about me.”*

Dana values low-pressure connection, practical resources, and events where family or friends can participate. They are sensitive to pity language and political symbolism.

**Goals:** Belonging, fun, useful connections, a welcoming entry point.  
**Frustrations:** Veteran stereotypes, insider jargon, crowded or unclear environments.  
**Needs:** Accessibility details, newcomer guidance, respectful storytelling.  
**Triggers:** Veteran-founded leadership, friends, clear welcome language.  
**Hesitations:** Partisan tone, trauma-forward appeals, unclear accommodations.  
**Channels:** Facebook, veteran networks, community calendars, local media.  
**RWC role:** Shared activity and connection without forced disclosure.

### Priya, 39 — Community partnerships manager, regional employer

*“Show me the audience, the activation, the safeguards, and the impact we can substantiate.”*

Priya manages community investment and needs an opportunity that employees and leadership can understand. She needs evidence, professional follow-through, and correct brand usage.

**Goals:** Credible local visibility; employee engagement; measurable support.  
**Frustrations:** Vague sponsor tiers, missing impact reports, last-minute asset requests.  
**Needs:** Sponsor deck, audience profile, deliverables, beneficiary and post-event report.  
**Triggers:** Clear packages, named beneficiary, photos, prior partners.  
**Hesitations:** Conflicting public information or unsupported nonprofit claims.  
**Channels:** LinkedIn, chamber networks, email, referrals, local media.  
**RWC role:** A tangible, participatory veteran-community activation.

### Eli, 26 — New tabletop player, Purdue/Greater Lafayette

*“I’m interested, but I don’t want to show up and discover everyone expects me to know everything.”*

Eli represents the growth audience: curious, socially motivated, unfamiliar with tournament norms, and likely to enter through a friend, demo, booth, or beginner guide.

**Goals:** Learn, meet people, have a good first experience.  
**Frustrations:** Gatekeeping and undefined jargon.  
**Needs:** “Start here” content, what-to-bring guidance, low-stakes entry options.  
**Channels:** Instagram/YouTube if established, campus/community groups, search, local events.  
**RWC role:** A welcoming path into play and purpose.

## 04 — Audience language

**Problem language:** “Is this per player or per team?” “What do I need to bring?” “Is registration actually open?” “Where does the money go?” “I want a community, not politics.”

**Desired outcome:** “A great time with great people.” “Well run.” “Worth the drive.” “Everyone leaves with something.” “Support a good cause.” These phrases reflect existing testimonials and event language; exact quotations should remain attributed.

**Implication:** Lead event messaging with logistics and experience, then connect it to mission. Lead sponsor messaging with audience, activation, accountability, and evidence. Lead veteran-resource content with usefulness and dignity.

## 05 — Not for

- People seeking a partisan or ideological veterans platform.
- Players who treat gatekeeping, harassment, or exclusion as part of competition.
- Sponsors seeking military imagery without meaningful participation or accountability.
- People requiring clinical, crisis, housing, or benefits services directly from RWC; route them to qualified resources.

## 06 — Brand implications

**Messaging:** “A tournament worth attending; a community worth joining; a mission worth supporting.”  
**Channels:** HCUnits and Facebook for conversion; store/podcast/community partnerships for discovery; email for retention; LinkedIn for sponsors.  
**Voice:** Game-literate, newcomer-friendly, veteran-respectful, operationally precise.  
**Experience:** One source of event truth, beginner guidance, accessibility information, sponsor proof, post-event reporting, and a repeatable retention path.
