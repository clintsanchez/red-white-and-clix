# Imagery disclosure — what changed on Terms and Disclaimer

Decision 2026-09-25: **the AI-generated hero figure stays.** These edits make
the legal pages describe that accurately instead of gesturing at it.

## What is actually on the site

Audited rather than assumed:

| Image | What it really is |
|---|---|
| `/uploads/rwc/hero-figure.webp` | **The only AI-generated image on the site.** An original armoured figure on a dial. Not a real person, not a published miniature, not a licensed character. |
| `/uploads/rwc/card-event.webp` | A **real photograph** of a real event. Roughly a dozen identifiable faces. |
| `/uploads/rwc/card-community.webp` | A **real photograph**. Roughly 25 identifiable faces. |
| `shop-*` product images | Printify **supplier mockups** — a design on a blank garment. |

## The false claim that was fixed

The Terms said AI imagery "is labeled as such where it appears." **It is not.**
The hero carries no visible label and no caption. That sentence was untrue the
day it was written. Replaced with a pointer to the Disclaimer, which does
identify it.

## New wording

**Terms → "Our content, and other people's"** now reads, in part:

> One image on this site — the figure on our home page — is digitally created
> rather than photographed. It is identified on our Website Disclaimer.
> Everything else that looks like a photograph of our events is a real
> photograph.

**Disclaimer → heading changed** from "Some imagery is AI-generated" to
**"The figure on our home page is AI-generated"**, because exactly one image is,
and a vague "some" invites the reader to distrust the event photographs too.
The body now reads:

> The armoured figure standing on a dial on our home page is digitally created.
> It is an original design, not a photograph, not a real person, and not any
> published miniature or licensed character. We use it because it is ours to use
> and it puts nobody on the page who did not agree to be there.
>
> Everything else is what it looks like. The event pictures on this site are
> real photographs taken at our events, and we never present a created image as
> a real moment. Merchandise pictures are supplier mockups showing a design on a
> blank garment, so the exact shade and fit of what arrives may differ slightly.

**Privacy → new "Photographs of people" section** giving anyone in an event
photo a no-questions-asked removal route.

## The thing this does not fix

That removal route is the *reactive* half. The proactive half — **asking the
people in those photographs whether they agreed to be on a public website** —
has still never been done. Two real photographs containing ~35 identifiable
faces are on the staging homepage now. This is a bigger exposure than the AI
hero ever was, and it is tracked in `CLIENT-FOLLOWUP.md` item 9.
