# Red White and Clix — brand story

## Long-form story

Every tabletop game begins the same way: people take a seat, set out the pieces and agree to share the experience. The competition matters, but so does everything around it—the conversation, the anticipation, the rematch and the people who remember your name when you return.

Red White and Clix was built around that kind of table.

Founder Wesley Robertson served in the Indiana Army National Guard, including a deployment to Mosul in 2004. His public story includes difficult chapters after service, friends lost to suicide and the support he received from a veterans organization when he needed it. Those experiences shaped a clear conviction: support is strongest when people can take part in it together.

Tabletop gaming offered a natural place to begin. HeroClix combines strategy, collecting and competition, but its lasting power comes from community. Players meet across a map, learn how one another thinks and come back for the relationships as much as the result. Red White and Clix turns that familiar experience into a practical invitation: play together and stand with veterans.

The organization’s events are designed for serious players and welcoming enough for someone still learning the format. A good event should be organized, fair and worth the trip. It should also make room for conversation and connection. Veterans, military families, longtime players, newcomers, sponsors and neighbors can participate in different ways while sharing one purpose.

That purpose is veteran support, approached with respect and transparency. Red White and Clix does not define veterans by hardship, and it does not use their stories as decoration. It creates an active community, raises awareness and supports veteran-focused organizations through events and giving. As the organization grows, its responsibility is to show that impact clearly: name the partners, verify the facts and report what participation made possible.

The name itself carries the idea. “Red White and Clix” links a familiar expression of American service with the game that brings the community together. It is memorable, specific to the category and rooted in the organization’s history. The brand does not belong to a political side. Its table is open to people who can compete fairly, treat others with respect and support the shared mission.

The story also carries a family legacy. Wesley’s public account honors his father, Major Edwin C. Robertson, whose example remains part of the organization’s foundation. That history gives the work emotional weight, but the brand’s future will be measured by what it builds now: reliable events, lasting relationships and a credible path for more people to participate.

Red White and Clix is still an emerging organization. Its strength is not institutional scale. Its strength is proximity. The founder is present. The players know one another. The event photographs show real tables and real participation. Partners can see where they fit. That local, human character should remain visible as the organization expands across Indiana and the Midwest.

The ambition is straightforward: become the region’s most trusted veteran-founded tabletop event community. Trust will come from doing the practical things well—publishing accurate details, creating a good player experience, welcoming newcomers, keeping commitments to partners and documenting impact without exaggeration.

At a Red White and Clix event, play is the entry point. Belonging is the experience. Service is the purpose. One table can become a community, and a community can choose to stand with veterans.

## Short version

Red White and Clix grew from founder Wesley Robertson’s military service, personal experience and belief in the community created around a tabletop game. Through welcoming, well-run HeroClix events, the organization brings players, veterans, families and supporters together to compete, connect and support veteran-focused work. The goal is simple: make participation meaningful, report impact honestly and give more people a seat at the table.

## Founder introduction
