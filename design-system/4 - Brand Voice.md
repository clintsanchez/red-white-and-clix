# Red White and Clix — voice guide

## Voice in one line

An organized host who welcomes every player, respects veterans and makes the next step clear.

## Core traits

**Welcoming:** invite people in without insider tests. **Grounded:** connect claims to dates, events and documented impact. **Energetic:** celebrate play and participation without hype. **Respectful:** treat veteran stories as human stories, never emotional props. **Practical:** lead with what, when, where and how.

## Tone by moment

| Moment | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event promotion | Upbeat and specific | “Join us November 7 for 300 Modern in Lafayette.” |
| New-player help | Patient and plain | “New to HeroClix? We’ll help you find the right format.” |
| Veteran mission | Warm and factual | “Your participation helps us support veteran-focused work.” |
| Donation | Transparent | “See what your gift supports before you donate.” |
| Problem or change | Direct and calm | “The start time changed to 10 a.m. Here’s the updated schedule.” |
| Memorial | Restrained and personal | “We remember the people who shaped this mission.” |

## Writing rules

Use active voice, short paragraphs and concrete verbs. Put event details before flourish. Say “Red White and Clix” on first reference and “RWC” only after defining it. Preserve “HeroClix.” Use contractions where natural. Define tournament terms for newcomers. Use one clear call to action per message.

## Words to use

Play, join, welcome, table, community, camaraderie, compete, participate, veterans, support, sponsor, volunteer, transparent, together.

## Words and claims to avoid

Avoid partisan slogans, combat metaphors for routine marketing, pity language, “real gamers,” “broken veterans,” guaranteed outcomes, medical claims, official military endorsement, invented impact totals and “every dollar goes directly” unless audited evidence supports it.

## Before and after

- Before: “CLICK HERE NOW FOR AN EPIC BATTLE!” After: “Register for the November 7 Modern event.”
- Before: “Help us save veterans.” After: “Play, volunteer or give in support of veteran-focused programs.”
- Before: “Everyone knows how sealed works.” After: “In Team Sealed, three players build and compete as a team.”
- Before: “Your entry changes lives.” After: “Your entry supports the event and our veteran-focused mission; we publish confirmed beneficiary details separately.”

## Channel guidance

Website copy explains the mission and removes registration friction. Social copy leads with a compelling moment, then the date and link. Email gives one useful update and one action. Sponsor materials pair community reach with verified deliverables. Crisis-resource copy stays direct, current and separate from promotional messaging.
