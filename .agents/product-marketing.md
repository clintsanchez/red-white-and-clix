# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v3  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-21

## Product Overview

**One-liner:** Veteran-founded tabletop events where competition, camaraderie and veteran support share the same table.  
**What it does:** Red White and Clix (RWC) organizes HeroClix events that bring players, veterans, families and supporters together while raising awareness and support for veteran-focused causes.  
**Product category:** Tabletop tournament organizer; community fundraising event; veteran-support nonprofit.  
**Product type:** Nonprofit events and community organization.  
**Business model:** Registration fees, donations, sponsorships and merchandise. Current authoritative pricing and purchase routes require reconciliation.

## Target Audience

**Primary segments:** Midwest HeroClix players; veterans and military families interested in gaming/community; local and regional sponsors; supportive community members.  
**Decision-makers:** Individual players; parents/partners for travel; sponsor owners and marketing/community-relations leads; donors.  
**Primary use case:** Attend a credible, welcoming HeroClix event with competitive value and a meaningful veteran-focused purpose.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Find an organized event worth the time, travel and entry fee.
- Compete and connect without an exclusionary environment.
- Support veterans through a concrete activity rather than a donation-only appeal.
- Give a local business a visible, accountable sponsorship opportunity.

## Personas

| Persona | Cares about | Challenge | Value promised |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive player | Format, rulings, prizes, attendance | Incomplete or conflicting logistics | A serious, clearly run event |
| Veteran/casual player | Belonging, respect, low social friction | Unfamiliar groups or intense competition | A welcoming table and shared purpose |
| New/returning player | Teaching, affordability, confidence | Rules, collection cost, fear of being outmatched | A clear path into play |
| Sponsor lead | Community relevance, visibility, proof | Unclear deliverables or impact | Defined participation and documented outcomes |

These are provisional until RWC obtains at least five first-party data points per segment.

## Problems & Pain Points

**Core problem:** Community members lack a single trusted path that combines a worthwhile tabletop event, welcoming culture and understandable veteran impact.  
**Alternatives fall short:** Local events may offer play without a cause; large veteran nonprofits offer impact without tabletop participation; gaming charities may not be local, tabletop-specific or veteran-focused.  
**Costs:** Missed registration, travel uncertainty, newcomer intimidation, sponsor hesitation and donor distrust.  
**Emotional tension:** “Will this be worth the trip?”, “Will I fit in?”, and “Where does the money go?”

## Competitive Landscape

**Competitor definition:** Organizations that serve veterans and compete for veteran-support attention, donations, volunteers, sponsors or community participation.  
**Primary benchmark and closest competitor:** Stack Up is the organization the founder initially wanted to emulate. It owns gaming plus veteran support and provides the clearest reference for community programs, participation paths and transparency.  
**Other competitors:** Operation Gratitude and Wounded Warrior Project.  
**Adjacent references, not competitors:** HeroClix tournaments and gaming fundraisers serving other causes may inform event mechanics, but they are outside the client-defined competitive set.

## Differentiation

- Veteran-founded, HeroClix-specific and locally rooted.
- Participation-led: people play, volunteer, sponsor or give.
- Serious competition paired with community welcome.
- Human-scale founder presence and authentic event imagery.

The advantage depends on accurate logistics and transparent impact reporting.

RWC should learn from Stack Up's model without imitating its national video-gaming breadth. The strategic translation is to adopt its clarity, program discipline and transparent proof, then specialize them for tabletop events, HeroClix and a Midwest community.

## Objections

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “Is the event worth the travel and fee?” | Publish the complete format, schedule, venue, prize and registration details in one place. |
| “I’m new or don’t have a competitive collection.” | Offer beginner guidance, loaner options or an explicitly welcoming side activity when operationally confirmed. |
| “Where does my money go?” | Name the beneficiary, explain allocation and publish confirmed results. |
| “Is this political?” | State clearly that RWC is nonpartisan and open to respectful participants. |

**Anti-persona:** People seeking partisan advocacy, harassment-tolerant competition, unsupported financial claims or guaranteed clinical outcomes.

## Switching Dynamics

**Push:** Fragmented event details, impersonal fundraisers, isolated play and stale local listings.  
**Pull:** A destination event, real camaraderie, founder credibility and visible veteran purpose.  
**Habit:** Familiar local venues, existing tournament calendars and donation routines.  
**Anxiety:** Travel, cost, skill gap, event quality and uncertainty about impact.

## Customer Language

Public HeroClix discussions emphasize “open, welcoming,” “helpful,” “patient,” “learn the basics,” “have fun,” “community,” “competitive,” “cost,” and “rules.” These are category-level observations, not RWC testimonials.

**Use:** seat at the table, play together, welcoming competition, clear format, camaraderie, support veterans.  
**Avoid:** save veterans, real gamers, guaranteed impact, official military endorsement, partisan slogans.
