# Red White and Clix — customer research synthesis

**Generated:** 2026-09-21  
**Modes:** Existing-asset analysis, public signal mining, primary-research design  
**Evidence boundary:** RWC has one client intake and public organizational content but no supplied attendee interview set, registration survey export or review corpus. Category insights below are hypotheses until validated with RWC participants.

## Sources and sample

- RWC GHL onboarding submission, website, Facebook and HCUnits listings.
- Seven independent public HeroClix Reddit threads concerning newcomers, local community, tournaments and competitive play (2022–2026).
- Official Stack Up, Operation Gratitude and Wounded Warrior Project pages. Extra Life was reviewed only as an adjacent participation reference and is not classified as a competitor.
- Founder direction that Stack Up was the organization he initially wanted RWC to emulate.

Online community posts skew toward engaged hobbyists and stronger opinions. They do not represent every player, veteran or donor.

## Top themes

### 1. Welcome and patient teaching lower the barrier

**Frequency:** 5 of 7 HeroClix threads. **Intensity:** High. **Confidence:** High for the category; low for RWC specifically.

Players repeatedly value organizers and regulars who teach rules, answer questions patiently and help someone enter play without already owning a collection. Implication: make newcomer support visible before registration and operationally real at the event.

### 2. Community is part of the product

**Frequency:** 6 of 7 threads. **Intensity:** High. **Confidence:** High for the category.

Participants describe helpful people and continued local play as central benefits, sometimes above prizes or results. Implication: sell the quality of the gathering alongside the tournament specification.

### 3. Competitive intensity can attract and intimidate

**Frequency:** 4 of 7 threads. **Intensity:** Medium-high. **Confidence:** Medium.

Players distinguish competitive HeroClix from casual fun and sometimes describe the tournament scene as elitist or expensive. Implication: label formats and expectations clearly; provide a low-risk newcomer entry point instead of claiming one experience fits everyone.

### 4. Cost and complexity create hesitation

**Frequency:** 4 of 7 threads. **Intensity:** Medium. **Confidence:** Medium.

Collection expense, rules knowledge and unfamiliar formats appear as recurring barriers. RWC’s conflicting public prices compound that category friction. Implication: publish one authoritative fee, what it includes and any loaner/learning support that actually exists.

### 5. A purpose beyond play can motivate participation

**Frequency:** Limited category evidence; present in charitable-event discussions and the RWC intake. **Confidence:** Low-medium.

Gaming communities do participate in cause-based events, but RWC has not supplied first-party evidence showing whether veteran impact, competition, prizes, founder affinity or social connection is the dominant registration trigger. Test rather than assume.

## Jobs to be done

| Segment | Functional job | Emotional job | Social job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competitive player | Find a credible tournament | Feel the trip and fee were worthwhile | Be recognized as a serious player |
| New/returning player | Learn and participate | Feel capable and welcome | Join the local hobby community |
| Veteran/family participant | Find a respectful shared activity | Experience camaraderie without pressure | Participate as a peer, not a symbol |
| Sponsor/donor | Support a credible local cause | Feel confident in the use of support | Demonstrate community commitment |

## Likely trigger events

- Annual event dates are announced and registration opens.
- A friend or local venue shares the event.
- A player wants higher-level competition or a destination weekend.
- A veteran or family member seeks a shared-interest community.
- A business allocates community sponsorship funds.

These triggers are hypotheses pending registration-source and interview data.

## Implications

1. Put date, place, format, fee and authoritative registration link together.
2. Describe newcomer experience concretely; do not make an unsupported “all skill levels” promise.
3. Use authentic participant images and specific logistics as proof.
4. Explain beneficiary and fund allocation before asking for a donation.
5. Segment competitive-player messaging from veteran-support and sponsor messaging.
6. Use Stack Up as the primary benchmark for program clarity, community participation and public transparency; validate which parts of its model the founder wants to reproduce operationally.

## Confidence ledger

| Price inconsistency harms trust | Medium | Direct public conflict; impact not measured |
| Sponsors require clearer deliverables and impact | Medium | Standard decision need plus intake goals; no sponsor interviews |

## Research gaps

- Why past attendees registered, returned or did not return.
- Registration discovery source and decision timeline.
- Relative importance of format, prizes, mission, people, venue and price.
- Newcomer participation and unmet support needs.
- Sponsor renewal drivers and required proof.
- Donation motivation and confidence barriers.
- Actual geographic draw and travel constraints.
