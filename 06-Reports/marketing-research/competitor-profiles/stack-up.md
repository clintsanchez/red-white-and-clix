## At a glance

Founded in 2015, Stack Up connects U.S. and allied service members, veterans and civilian supporters through video gaming. Its four named programs are The Stacks, Supply Crates, Air Assaults and the Stack Up Overwatch Program (StOP).

**Relationship to RWC:** Primary aspirational benchmark. Wesley Robertson identified Stack Up as the organization he initially wanted Red White and Clix to emulate.

## Positioning and messaging

**Value proposition:** Veteran support and connection through the shared language of gaming.  
**Audience:** Service members, veterans, gamers, donors, volunteers and gaming-industry partners.  
**Angle:** Gaming itself is a credible channel for community, morale and support.

## Offer and proof

Stack Up pairs local/community chapters with tangible programs, event experiences, gaming shipments and crisis support. It publishes annual reports, audits, tax records and determination documents on a dedicated transparency page.

## Strengths

- Owns the closest conceptual territory to RWC: gaming plus veteran support.
- National reach and a mature program portfolio.
- Strong transparency infrastructure.
- Multiple participation routes beyond donation.

## Weaknesses and gaps

- Broad video-gaming focus leaves room for a tabletop and HeroClix specialist.
- National scale can feel less locally personal.
- Its multi-program model is more complex than RWC’s event-led proposition.

## Implications for RWC

RWC should not claim the broad territory “gaming helps veterans.” It can own **veteran-founded tabletop events in the Midwest**, with founder presence, serious HeroClix play and local beneficiary proof. Stack Up establishes the transparency standard RWC should emulate.

The useful model is structural rather than visual imitation: define programs clearly, offer several concrete ways to participate, publish annual impact and financial evidence, and make gaming integral to the mission. RWC should translate those practices into a smaller tabletop-event system.
